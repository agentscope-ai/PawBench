# Navigating Product Launches in an Open-Source and Continuous Delivery Model: Insights from GitLab's Product Marketing Team

## Introduction

In today's fast-paced tech landscape, product marketing teams face the unique challenge of announcing new features and improvements when their company operates on a continuous delivery model with an open-source roadmap. This approach, while beneficial for fostering community engagement and rapid innovation, can complicate the traditional 'big-bang' release strategy. Drawing on insights from a recent meeting of GitLab's Product Marketing team, this blog post will explore how to effectively communicate product launches in such an environment.

## The Challenge of Public Roadmaps and Incremental Releases

GitLab, like many other companies that embrace transparency and open-source development, has a completely public roadmap. This means that every planned feature, enhancement, and bug fix is visible to the community before it even reaches the first iteration. Additionally, the practice of shipping Minimum Viable Changes (MVCs) or incremental updates rather than waiting for a fully polished feature set adds another layer of complexity. These small, frequent releases can make it difficult to create impactful, cohesive announcements that capture the attention of the press and the broader market.

### Strategy 1: Grouping Related Features into Thematic Announcements

One effective strategy discussed by the GitLab team is to group related MVCs and minor enhancements into thematic announcements. For example, instead of announcing each individual improvement to vulnerability management, the team might highlight the overall progress made over the past year. This approach allows for a more compelling narrative, showcasing the cumulative value delivered to users. By focusing on the broader impact and the journey from initial concept to a robust, user-friendly feature, you can provide a more engaging story for your audience.

### Strategy 2: Leveraging Major Release Milestones

Another key lesson is to leverage major version releases as platforms for broader announcements. Even if the actual changes are iterative, a major release number (e.g., moving from version 13.0 to 14.0) can serve as a focal point for summarizing the most significant advancements since the last major release. This not only provides a natural opportunity to reframe and repackage the ongoing work but also aligns with the expectations of the market and media for periodic, substantial updates. During these milestones, consider highlighting the most transformative features, sharing success stories, and emphasizing the value proposition for both existing and potential customers.

### Strategy 3: Focusing on User-Centric Benefits

When communicating about incremental releases, it's crucial to focus on the benefits they bring to the end-user. Instead of merely listing technical details, articulate how these changes improve the user experience, enhance security, or streamline workflows. A user-centric communication strategy helps in maintaining relevance and interest, making it easier to engage with the target audience. It also aids in building a stronger connection between the product and its users, reinforcing the value of continuous improvement.

## Conclusion

Navigating product launches in an environment of continuous delivery and open-source development requires a strategic and thoughtful approach. By grouping related features, leveraging major release milestones, and focusing on the tangible benefits to users, product marketing teams can successfully communicate the value of their ongoing work. These strategies, inspired by the experiences of GitLab's Product Marketing team, offer practical guidance for creating impactful and resonant announcements, even in the context of incremental and transparent development processes.
